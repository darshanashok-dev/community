0 of 6 recovery steps marked complete (for your reference only).

  1. 1

    Call 1930 immediately for financial cyber fraud reporting

    Dial India's 24/7 toll-free National Cyber Crime Helpline 1930 as soon as possible if money was transferred or banking credentials were exposed. Helpline operators log the transaction in the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System (CFCFRMS) to alert banks and attempt an urgent freeze.

  2. 2

    Report through cybercrime.gov.in

    File a formal complaint on the official Government of India (I4C / MHA) portal cybercrime.gov.in under "File a Complaint". Complete OTP verification, enter exact transaction and caller details, upload screenshots, and save your acknowledgement number.

  3. 3

    Contact your bank immediately

    Call your bank's official helpline (printed on the back of your debit/credit card or inside your official banking app—never from a search-engine ad). Request temporary blocking of compromised cards, UPI IDs, or net banking, and record your bank complaint/ticket reference.

  4. 4

    Preserve transaction records

    Keep copies of UTR / RRN reference numbers, exact transaction amounts, date and time stamps, recipient account numbers or UPI IDs, and bank SMS/email alerts.

  5. 5

    Retain relevant messages and evidence

    Do not delete chats or call logs yet. Save screenshots of SMS messages, WhatsApp threads, caller phone numbers, Skype/video-call usernames, suspicious links, and any forged PDF warrants or IDs. If an untrusted APK or remote-access app was installed, disconnect from the internet and remove it safely before changing passwords from a clean device.

  6. 6

    Follow up through appropriate official channels

    Track your complaint status on cybercrime.gov.in using your acknowledgement number and coordinate with your local cybercrime police station or bank grievance officer if requested. Beware of secondary scams: nobody online can recover lost money for an upfront fee.
